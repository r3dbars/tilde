# How We Type on Smartphones (Palin, Feit, et al., CHI 2019)

**Source:** https://userinterfaces.aalto.fi/typing19/ (paper: often cited as Palin et al., CHI 2019)
**Related dataset lineage:** the 136-million-keystroke desktop study (already digested).
**License:** Project page / ACM. Link and attribute.

## What it does (plain words)

The phone-keyboard sibling of Dhakal's 136-million-keystroke paper. Thousands of people typed on their own phones. The study measured speed, errors, autocorrect, and suggestion use in the wild-ish conditions of a mobile typing test.

## Method

Web/mobile typing test, own device, transcription of sentences. They report WPM, error rates, and how often people took the keyboard's helpers. This is the paper later suggestion studies cite when they say mobile suggestions are "used every 63 characters" or similarly rarely.

## Key findings

- Mobile typing is slower and more error-prone than desktop. Helpers exist because the channel is bad.
- Suggestions and autocorrect are used, but much less than vendors imply.
- Device and uncorrected errors dominate. A good model cannot make a soft keyboard as fast as a hardware keyboard.

## What Tilde should take from it

Tilde is not this product. Desktop physical keyboards are already fast (Roy's 76.5 WPM). Importing mobile-keyboard aggressiveness (always-on bar, autocorrect) would be solving a problem the owner does not have.

Use Palin as the "why phones shout suggestions" paper, and Roy/Dhakal as the "why a Mac IME should whisper" pair.

## Limits and caveats

Transcription test, not composition. Vendor keyboards differ. Exact suggestion-use numbers depend on the year and the keyboard. Do not treat a mobile frequency as a Tilde show-rate target.
