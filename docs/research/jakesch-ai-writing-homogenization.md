# AI writing flattens language (Jakesch, Bhat, Buschek, and related)

**Sources:**
- Jakesch, Bhat, Buschek, et al. on how AI-mediated replies change perceived language (search: "AI-mediated communication homogenization Jakesch")
- Related: Arnold 2020 (already digested); CoAuthor / Lee et al. 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06796
**License:** ACM / arXiv depending on the paper. Link and attribute. Do not quote participant writing.

## What it does (plain words)

When an assistant offers likely next phrases, the resulting corpus looks more like the model's prior: shorter, more common words, more similar across people. Jakesch's line of work measures that social effect. Lee's CoAuthor logs how people and GPT-3 take turns and how control feels.

## Method

Controlled writing tasks with and without model suggestions or insertions. They compare lexical diversity, sentiment, and human judgments of authorship or trust. CoAuthor is a large interaction dataset (public, not Tilde's owner data).

## Key findings

- Suggestion systems can move both the process and the product toward the average.
- People still feel faster or more fluent while that happens.
- Co-writing UIs that *insert* or offer long takes change ownership more than a one-word prediction.

## Limits and caveats

Chat and essay tasks, often with models that talk in complete paragraphs. Tilde's three-word ghost is a weaker steer and still not zero. These papers do not give an RNKS-compatible agency metric; they give the reason to keep looking for one.
