# Tilde — brand system v1

Locked 2026-07-29 from the owner's moodboard (moodboard-v1.png) and icon
(icon-v1.png). This file is the durable reference; the images are the source.

## The mark

A single tilde stroke above a short straight underline.

- The tilde is the writer's voice: one crest, one midline crossing, one
  trough, aligned endpoints, 180° rotational balance (see moodboard panel 2).
- The underline is the input field — the machine's line the voice rides above.
- Together they echo ≃ ("almost equal"): the almost-said sentence. That pun
  is the product promise in one glyph.

## Color

- Ink: near-black on paper off-white. That's the brand; there are two colors.
- Accent: one blue, used ONLY for the underline element and suggestion
  affordances. Never a second accent.
- Menu bar: TEMPLATE MONO (macOS convention) — the tilde alone or
  tilde+underline in monochrome. The blue underline lives in the dock icon,
  the wordmark, and the product; never the status bar.

## Type

- Wordmark and headlines: a warm serif ("Tilde" set like a word in a book,
  not a logo). On-platform: New York fits native-minimal.
- UI and body: system (SF). The product itself never uses the serif.

## The suggestion grammar (product = brand)

From moodboard panels 5-6 — the states ARE the identity:

- SUGGESTED: ghost text in secondary gray with the blue underline.
- ACCEPTED: text becomes plain ink; underline gone.
- DISMISSED: nothing left behind.

Note: IME marked text is conventionally underlined by macOS — the brand
turns a platform artifact into the product's signature. The per-app styling
matrix work should implement exactly this grammar.

## Voice

- Site headline: "Write with momentum."
- Story sentence: "Tilde finishes your sentences in your own voice — and
  your words never leave your Mac."
- Tagline (comparative): "Autocomplete writes like everyone. Tilde writes
  like you."
- Sub: "Suggestions that feel like your next thought."

## Character, not mascot

The glyph is the character (owner decision 2026-07-29): personality lives in
the stroke's gesture — a wave, a settle — never eyes, face, or limbs. Usable
in onboarding (one wave when the keyboard comes alive), empty states, About.
Never on the typing surface.
